The Genesis of the Colombo School of Philosophy

The Colombo School of Philosophy began in 2012 not as a formal institution, but as a quiet rupture—a small, dedicated reading group convening at the University of Colombo. Driven by a desire to move beyond established post-structuralist paradigms, the group sought to engage directly with the dramatic shifts reshaping contemporary continental thought: the rise of new materialisms, speculative realisms, and the broader intellectual movement that has come to be known as the Speculative Turn.

Over the past decade, this intellectual experiment evolved through vibrant partnerships across Colombo’s cultural and educational landscape. Moving to the Digital Film Academy at the Sri Lanka Foundation Institute, the group undertook a rigorous, line-by-line reading of Heidegger’s Being and Time, interrogating the foundations of phenomenology and correlationism. The journey continued at the Theertha School of Arts—supported by the Theertha International Artists’ Collective—where philosophical enquiry intersected with artistic practice, critical theory, and local political reality.

By 2024, after years of sustained labor—ranging from close readings of foundational texts (Kant's first Critique, Descartes's Meditations, Lyotard, and Derrida) to translating contemporary speculative thought into local contexts—the collective officially registered as the Colombo School of Philosophy.

Today, the School stands as an active intellectual workshop in Sri Lanka. It remains committed to rethinking the relationship between mind, matter, and existence—offering a space where radical non-correlationist thought meets the ongoing demand for local cultural and social transformation.
